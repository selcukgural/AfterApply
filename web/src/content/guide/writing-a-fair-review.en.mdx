What you write about a company may be the most honest thing a candidate gets to see. The job ad is the company describing itself; your review is what it looks like from inside. That is why short and real beats long and general.

> Sharing a negative experience is fine. What matters is that you tell it respectfully, honestly and from your own experience.

## Do this

- **Write about your own experience.** What you lived through, not what you heard.
- **Be honest.** Positive or negative — both are valuable, both get published.
- **Be specific.** Instead of "it was bad", say what happened: when, how often, how.
- **Give the context.** Which period, what kind of team — so a reader can compare it with their own situation.

## Avoid this

- **Don't target individuals.** No names, initials or descriptions that give someone away. You can name the role ("the manager I reported to"), not the person.
- **No insults, profanity or threats.** These don't get a review published; they get it sent back.
- **No personal information.** Yours or anyone else's — phone numbers, e-mail, addresses, ID numbers.
- **No serious accusations you can't back up.** Tell what happened to you; "the company does X" claims don't belong here.
- **No confidential company information.** Client names, contract values, internal messages.
- **No ads, spam or manipulation.** No links, job postings, or praise and criticism written to order.

## Same experience, two ways to write it

Each pair below says the same thing. The first gets sent back or tells nobody anything; the second actually gives the reader something.

**Not this:** "Management is terrible, nobody knows anything."\
**This:** "For two years the team's goals changed mid-quarter; we often learned what we'd be working on at the last minute."

**Not this:** "Stay away from sales manager A. K., he shouts at everyone."\
**This:** "Communication with the manager I reported to was hard; feedback mostly came in meetings, in front of the team."

**Not this:** "They say the pay is good but it's a lie, the company is going under anyway."\
**This:** "Pay was slightly below market, but raises came every year without fail; there was a meal and commute allowance."

**Not this:** "Great company, I recommend it to everyone!!!"\
**This:** "I was assigned a mentor in the first three months and weekly one-on-ones actually happened. Work really did end at 6pm."
