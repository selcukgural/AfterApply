The ratings you give a company may be the most honest thing someone weighing that company ever sees. The job ad is where the company describes itself; your review is what it looks like from inside. There is nothing to write here: you rate, and you pick from ready-made statements that were written to be fair to everyone.

> Sharing a negative experience is fine. A low rating is published too; the measure is not whether the company likes it, but whether you actually lived it.

## How the form works

- **Your relationship to the company and one overall rating** — that is all that is required. A rating runs from 1 (very negative) to 5 (very positive).
- **Ten more areas can be rated:** work environment, management, career, work-life balance, pay, benefits, remote work, tools, hiring, onboarding. Leave out any area you cannot judge — leaving it blank is not a "3"; it simply does not count toward that area's average.
- **Once you rate an area** the form offers a few statements about it: things you liked for a high rating, things that could be better for a low one. "More" shows the area's full list.
- **At most five "liked" and five "could be better" statements** — for the whole review, not per area. The limit is for the reader: five statements say something, thirty say nothing.
- **Published the moment you save.** No waiting. Readers can report it; if a moderator takes it down, the reason appears under [My Reviews](/my-reviews).

## Why there is no free text

Reviews here used to have a title, pros and cons. We removed them because moderators are not lawyers: even a sentence that looked reasonable could turn out to be a claim about a person, an insult or an accusation nobody could verify — with both the author and us standing behind it. Ready-made statements carry none of that: they name nobody, claim nothing, and describe an experience. You can see which statements exist on [any company page](/companies); the list is the same for everyone.

## Rating honestly

- **Rate your own experience.** What happened to you, not what you heard.
- **Keep the areas apart.** Pay can be poor while the team is good; giving both the same rating paints the reader a false picture.
- **Give the overall rating last.** "How was it as a whole" is easier to answer once you have thought about the parts.
- **Don't reach for the extremes** — unless it really was one. 1 and 5 are honest ratings too.

## The same experience, two ways to pick

**Not this:** a 1 in every area, five "could be better" statements, all about pay.\
**This:** pay 2 with "salary level" and "alignment with the market"; work environment 4 with "team communication". The reader sees what was good and what was not.

**Not this:** a 5 in every area, no statements.\
**This:** overall 4, remote work 5 with "flexible hours", career 3 with "promotion criteria". That is the one people believe.
